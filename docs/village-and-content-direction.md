# Unity Parks: the village, and everything we sell in it

*Status: proposed, 13 August 2026. The single source of truth for what Unity
Parks is, where it is, how the village is laid out, what a lodge is, and what
we sell across the year. Written as though the full village is built, because
you cannot design a coherent product from a twenty-lodge slice.*

*Supersedes the Lake Naivasha setting throughout. Companion to
docs/content-strategy.md (which decides how content is delivered and keeps its
four-season framework), docs/DESIGN.md (voice and visual system),
docs/deposit-and-cancellation-plan.md and docs/post-booking-extras.md (money
paths, unchanged by any of this). Section 15 lists what has to change in code.*

---

## 1. The decision in one page

Unity Parks is a car-free forest village on the western slopes of Mount Kenya,
built for families to spend time together, two and a half hours from Nairobi.

- **Place.** Naro Moru, Kieni West, Nyeri County. 150 acres at about 2,100 m,
  with the Mount Kenya forest reserve boundary as the eastern edge.
- **Anchor.** The Water Garden: a covered, heated water world, glazed toward
  the mountain. Entry is included in every break.
- **Lodges.** 180 at full build. Two sizes, two grades: Cedar and Signature, at
  2 and 3 bedrooms. Nothing else.
- **Grade is what the lodge has. Zone is where it sits.** These are sold
  separately and never confused.
- **The year has four seasons and three break shapes.** Weekend, midweek, full
  week. Demand is driven by school terms, not weather.
- **Breaks are bundled by who can travel, not by what the sky is doing.**

## 2. The place

The western foothills of Mount Kenya, in the Naro Moru and Burguret belt of
Kieni West, Nyeri County.

| | |
|---|---|
| Altitude | About 2,100 m |
| Site | 150 acres, forest reserve boundary to the east |
| From Nairobi | About 180 km, two and a half to three hours, tarmac throughout on the A2 |
| Nearest airstrip | Nanyuki, 30 km north |
| Rivers | The Burguret runs through the site, the Naro Moru to the south |
| Forest | Indigenous montane forest: African pencil cedar, podo, wild olive, fig, bamboo higher up |

Why this side of the mountain, and not the wetter southern slopes: from the
south and east the peaks sit in cloud most of the day, and the land is
smallholder tea and coffee country where assembling 150 contiguous acres would
take years. The western slopes are former ranch and large-farm country where
single-title parcels at this size are ordinary market stock. The site also sits
well clear of the Mount Kenya to Ngare Ndare elephant corridor, which is
protected connectivity for the World Heritage site and no place for a resort.

**The climate is the product.** Days are mild, nights are cold, and June to
September is genuinely chilly with mist in the mornings. That is why a covered
heated water world makes sense here in a way it never did at a lake, and why
fireplaces, hot tubs and heavy linen are honest rather than decorative.

**One truth worth using everywhere.** The mountain hides in cloud through the
middle of the day and shows itself at dawn and at sunset. An early breakfast is
an event.

## 3. What kind of park this is

> A car-free forest village on Mount Kenya. Lodges among the trees, warm water
> and forest trails at the door, two and a half hours from Nairobi.

That line goes under the logo and opens the village page. The competitive frame
behind it, which we state to ourselves and imply to guests:

- **Not a safari camp.** Wildlife is a neighbour here, not an itinerary. Nobody
  gets up at five to sit in a vehicle.
- **Not a coast hotel.** No flight, no packing for a week of heat, and it works
  in the months when the coast is a four-hour flight-and-transfer away.
- **Not a weekend resort with a lawn.** It is a village, with a centre, lanes,
  a shop and 180 front doors, and the children can cross it on their own
  because there are no cars in it.

The single promise underneath all of it: **you arrive, you park, and you do not
get back in the car until you leave.**

## 4. The village

### 4.1 Site logic

The land falls from the forest reserve boundary in the east down toward the
farmland in the west. Three consequences shape everything:

1. **The mountain is east.** East-facing plots get the peaks and the sunrise.
   That is the premium orientation, and it costs nothing to design for.
2. **Uphill is quieter, colder, more private, better viewed. Downhill is
   flatter, warmer, closer to everything.** So price rises as you climb.
3. **The Burguret runs north to south down the slope**, giving bridges, the
   sound of water, a fishing beat and a picnic meadow.

The eastern boundary carries an electrified game fence, because elephant and
buffalo genuinely move through that forest. The fence is not hidden. It is the
edge of the wild, and standing at it is one of the things you come for.

### 4.2 Arrival and the road

One gate, one road in. The Gatehouse reads your plate at the barrier, which is
why we capture it at booking. Cars go once to the lodge, unload, and stay put
for the break. Every lane loops. There is no through road anywhere in the
village, and no reason to drive inside it.

### 4.3 The Village Square

Low and central, within a ten-minute walk of every lodge.

- **The Water Garden.** Covered and heated, planted, glazed on the mountain
  side so you swim looking at the peaks. Warm water and green under a roof
  while it is cold outside. Slides, a lagoon, a toddler pool, lane swimming
  early. **Entry is included in every break**, because an anchor that is
  ticketed separately is not an anchor.
- **The outdoor pool and lagoon deck**, alongside, for the dry months.
- **The Market.** Groceries, essentials, firewood, the butcher's counter.
- **The Bakehouse.** Bread, coffee, pastries, from six in the morning.
- **The Long Table.** All-day family restaurant, high chairs everywhere, no
  booking needed.
- **Kirimara.** The evening restaurant, bookable, for the night the parents get
  an hour to themselves. Named for one of the mountain's own names, which is
  the one place in the village where a local name is doing real work.
- **The Barn.** Activity centre and bookings desk, bike hire, climbing wall,
  indoor courts, table tennis, the craft workshop for wet afternoons.
- **The Den.** Kids' club and soft play.

### 4.4 The Forest Spa

Deliberately set apart, uphill in the trees, ten minutes on foot from the
square. Adults only, and quiet is the rule. On a cold mountain a spa sells
heat: hot pools, sauna, steam room, treatment rooms, and outdoor tubs looking
at the peaks.

### 4.5 The forest edge

At the top of the site, along the fence: the trailhead, the canopy walkway, the
sunrise viewing deck, and the start of the guided night walk.

### 4.6 The zones

Four, each with a character, and each a real choice at the Location step.

| Zone | Where | Character | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Riverside** | Low, along the Burguret | Flattest, pushchair-easy, water sound, closest to the square | Families with small children |
| **The Glades** | Central, among the trees | Three minutes from the Water Garden | Short breaks, convenience |
| **Cedar Rise** | Mid slope, east | First real mountain views over the canopy | The upgrade sweet spot |
| **Sunrise Ridge** | Top, east, on the forest edge | Biggest plots, widest tree screens, tubs facing the peaks | Celebration, escape, long stays |

**Every zone carries both grades.** The Signature share rises as you climb, but
it never reaches 100 percent and never falls to zero. If a zone were single
grade, then zone and grade would be the same thing and the Location fee would
be charging twice for one decision.

Lodges sit in **lanes of four to six around a shared green**, not in rows. This
is what makes "neighbouring lodges" a promise we can keep for a family taking
three of them, and it is what turns the celebration break into a real product.

### 4.7 The numbers

180 lodges, 936 beds, on 150 acres.

| Type | Riverside | The Glades | Cedar Rise | Sunrise Ridge | Total | Beds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cedar 2 bedroom | 20 | 16 | 12 | 6 | **54** | 216 |
| Cedar 3 bedroom | 24 | 20 | 18 | 10 | **72** | 432 |
| Signature 2 bedroom | 2 | 3 | 5 | 8 | **18** | 72 |
| Signature 3 bedroom | 3 | 5 | 10 | 18 | **36** | 216 |
| **Zone total** | **49** | **44** | **45** | **42** | **180** | **936** |

Signature share by zone: 10 percent at Riverside, 18 at The Glades, 33 at Cedar
Rise, 62 at Sunrise Ridge.

The mix is weighted to three bedrooms and to the standard grade, because the
Kenyan family travels bigger and the premium buyer is the celebration buyer,
not the couple.

Land split: about 60 acres of lodge zones, 50 of retained forest and trails, 15
for the square, 10 for the spa and activities, 15 for back of house. Deliveries,
laundry, the staff village and waste have their own road that never crosses a
guest path, because the moment it does the car-free promise dies.

### 4.8 Phasing

| Phase | Lodges | What opens with it |
|---|---|---|
| One | 60 | The Water Garden, the square, The Long Table, The Market, The Barn, Riverside and The Glades |
| Two | 60 | The Forest Spa, Kirimara, the canopy walkway, Cedar Rise |
| Three | 60 | The team and conference building, Sunrise Ridge, the hero units |

The Water Garden is unavoidably front-loaded. It is the anchor and it cannot be
built in thirds.

## 5. The lodges

### 5.1 The model

Two axes and nothing else. **Size is a number. Grade is a name.**

| Apaleo code | Name | Bedrooms | Sleeps | Base per night |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WDL | Cedar Lodge 2 bedroom | 2 | 4 | KES 28,000 |
| FST | Cedar Lodge 3 bedroom | 3 | 6 | KES 42,000 |
| LKV | Signature Lodge 2 bedroom | 2 | 4 | KES 38,000 |
| EXC | Signature Lodge 3 bedroom | 3 | 6 | KES 56,000 |

The ladder explains itself out loud: a bedroom costs about half as much again,
and Signature costs about a third more. Base prices are the long-rains floor
(section 11).

Codes stay as they are. They are opaque, they appear in URLs, and changing them
means re-provisioning every unit, rate plan and rate for no guest-visible gain.

Parties of more than six take two lodges in the same lane. That is not a
workaround, it is the extended-family product.

### 5.2 Cedar, the base spec

Insulated and double-glazed. A wood-burning stove. Hot water that copes with a
full house at six in the morning. Full kitchen with oven, hob and
fridge-freezer. Made-up beds and towels on arrival. A private deck with a
built-in braai. Parking beside the lodge. Wi-Fi throughout. Cot and high chair
on request, at no charge.

**Every lodge gets a stove, Cedar included.** At 2,100 m heat is not a luxury,
and a fireplace in all 180 lodges is what sells firewood on the extras step.

### 5.3 Signature, the seven things

Identical in every Signature lodge, at both sizes.

| | Why it earns the premium here |
|---|---|
| Private outdoor hot tub, deck-mounted, facing the mountain | The hero item. On a cold mountain it is the strongest upgrade there is |
| Wraparound deck, roofed at one end, outdoor dining and a built-in braai | The roof is the Mount Kenya detail. The tub and the braai still work in the rains, which is when the soft weeks need selling |
| Underfloor heating in the bathrooms | Cheap, and the thing guests talk about on a cold morning |
| En-suite to the main bedroom plus a second bathroom | Removes the only real friction in a full lodge |
| Upgraded kitchen: dishwasher, full oven, coffee machine | Self-catering for a week is a different job from three nights |
| Robes, slippers, heavier linen, better beds | Low cost, high perceived value, reinforces the warmth story |
| Bigger plot with a wider tree screen | What "private" has to actually mean |

The through-line, said out loud: **Signature is the cold-weather grade.**

**The rule that keeps this clean: anything we sell as an extra must not be in
the grade.** No welcome hamper, because we sell a grocery pack. No firewood,
because we sell firewood. No spa pass. The grade is fabric bolted to the
building. Extras are consumables. Confusing the two cannibalises a revenue line
we have already built.

The 3 bedroom Signature gets **more of the same, never different things**:
bigger deck, bigger tub, the extra bathroom a larger party needs.

### 5.4 What varies and what never varies

| Never varies | Varies, and is sold separately |
|---|---|
| Everything in the grade spec | Slope, aspect, tree screen, how far to the square |
| The promise that Signature means a hot tub | Which zone, and which exact lodge in it |

Apaleo prices by unit group and assumes units within a group are
interchangeable. Honour that. If Signature lodges differed materially, a guest
could not know what they were getting without paying the Location fee, which
turns a delight into a lottery.

### 5.5 Naming and numbering

Guests navigate by place, not by product. Units are named for their lane and
number, not their tier: **Fig Lane 3**, not "Signature Lodge 3". Lane names come
from what grows and lives there: Cedar, Podo, Fig, Olive, Bamboo, Turaco,
Sunbird, Hyrax.

### 5.6 The hero units, phase three

Six one-off lodges, a fifth product with their own name and rate, not a
Signature variant. Candidates: canopy lodges in the trees, a stargazing lodge
with a retractable roof, one on the forest fence with its own viewing hide.
This is the brochure cover and it books eighteen months out. It stays out of
the booking engine until phase three, because a fifth unit group means fresh
provisioning and a fresh set of rate writes.

The rule from section 5.3 holds: extras are consumables and services, never

| Grocery welcome pack | The fridge full on arrival |
| Firewood and BBQ pack | Sold by every stove in every lodge |

| Service | Why |
|---|---|
| Butcher's box | Nyama choma is the truth of a Kenyan braai, and it is not the grocery pack |
| Road transfer from Nairobi | Three hours is a real journey and not everyone wants to drive it |
| Airstrip transfer | Nanyuki, for the fly-in guest |
| Baby kit | Bath, monitor, blackout blind, bottle steriliser. The cot and high chair stay free |
| Flex cancellation cover | A service fee, with the refund logic ours |
| Bike trailers and child seats | Attaches to cycle hire |
| Private chef | Phase three, operations-heavy |

**No late checkout.** The turnover rule fixes the dates: every departure
morning is another family's arrival day, Friday or Monday, and the hours
between checkout and check-in belong to housekeeping. Early check-in spends
slack in the afternoon; late checkout would sell the next guest's clean.

## 9. The year

Four seasons, each named with its months, every time. The words that describe
northern hemisphere weather do not exist here and never appear in our copy.

| Season | Months | The mountain in that season | Rate multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunshine season** | December to February | Hot clear days, cold nights, the peaks out at breakfast. The festive weeks and the long school break sit inside it. Our busiest | 1.30 |
| **Long rains** | March to May | Storms in the afternoon, clear mornings, the forest at its greenest, the best prices of the year. The April holidays sit inside it | 1.00 |
| **Cool season** | June to September | Mist to the knees at dawn, frost on the higher farms, fires lit by five, the Water Garden steaming. The August holidays sit in the middle | 1.15 |
| **Short rains** | October to November | Light afternoon showers, green everything, quiet trails, value before the rush | 1.08 |

**The cool season is the hero, not the shoulder.** Kenyan families go to the
coast in December and August. A mountain cannot win on sunshine, so it sells
what the coast has never had: cold mornings, hot water, and a fire. That is the
sharpest positioning we own, and June to September is where it lives.

**On rain, one honest line.** It rains in the afternoon here, not all day. The
mornings are usually clear, and the peaks show themselves best once rain has
washed the air.

## 10. Breaks

### 10.1 The three shapes

The turnover rule is a product, not a restriction. Name the shapes and the rule
sells itself.

| Break | Arrive | Nights | Depart |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weekend break** | Friday | 3 | Monday |
| **Midweek break** | Monday | 4 | Friday |
| **Full week** | Friday or Monday | 7 | |

A three-hour drive makes the full week the best value in the range and the
weekend the easiest sale. The midweek break is the hardest inventory in the
year, which is what section 10.3 is for.

### 10.2 Who can travel when

Demand here is driven by school terms, not by weather. Getting this right is
the whole of the merchandising plan.

| Window | Who can come | What sells |
|---|---|---|
| April, August, late October to early January | Families with school-age children | Nothing. These weeks sell themselves. Charge, do not discount |
| Term time, everything else | Couples, retirees, remote workers, corporates, families with pre-school children, groups of friends | The bundles below. Roughly half the year |
| Public holiday long weekends | Everyone, briefly | The Friday three-night break, named for the holiday |

The long rains are not the empty season people assume, because the April
holidays sit inside them. The genuinely soft weeks are term-time March, May,
early October, and June, July and September outside the August holiday.

Public holidays worth naming: Easter, Labour Day on 1 May, Madaraka Day on
1 June, Utamaduni Day on 10 October, Mashujaa Day on 20 October, Jamhuri Day on
12 December, and the two Eids. A Friday three-night break covers a Monday
holiday exactly.

### 10.3 The bundles

Three rules. **Bundle by who can travel, not by weather.** **Repackage
inventory we already have, never invent operations.** **Fill soft weeks with
value, not price cuts**, because discounting teaches a market to wait, and this
market already waits.

| Bundle | Window | For | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fireside break** | Term time, cool season and the rains | Couples, escapers | Firewood and BBQ pack, spa pass |
| **Work from the forest** | Term time, 7 nights | Remote workers, retirees, long stays | Week rate, desk and Wi-Fi, grocery pack |
| **Little ones break** | Term time, midweek | Families with pre-school children | Quiet village, toddler pool sessions, baby kit, kids' club |
| **Team break** | Term time, multi-lodge | Companies | Two or more lodges, meeting space, catering. Operations we do not yet have, so flag it honestly |
| **Long-weekend break** | Each public holiday | Everyone | The Friday three-night break, named for the holiday. No new operations at all |

The Little ones break deserves the attention: it targets the single hardest
inventory in the year, Monday to Thursday in term time, with the one family
segment that has no school calendar to obey.

### 10.4 The campaigns

Two named moments a year, sitting on top of the seasons rather than replacing
them.

- **The festive break.** Mid-December to early January. Fixed-length, a Friday
  or Monday start covering 25 December or 1 January, with an event evening,
  a tree in the square and lights through the forest. The peak, and the 1.5
  multiplier already lives here.
- **August by the fire.** The cool-season campaign in the middle of the long
  school holiday. Mist, stoves, hot tubs, the Water Garden steaming. The one
  the coast cannot answer.

Write both as evergreen pages with a changing window, not as dated campaigns.
The booking horizon is thirteen months (extended from 100 days on 17 August
2026), so every campaign in the year ahead is bookable, but the evergreen
rule stands: a dated page goes stale on the site, a window line does not.

### 10.5 What we do not sell

No snow. No autumn, spring or the seasons named for northern weather. No
manufactured winter of any kind. The equator is the point, and pretending
otherwise is the exact failure the four-season model was written to fix.

## 11. Prices

Base rates are the long-rains floor, per lodge per night, whatever the party
size. Seasonal multipliers from section 9 are applied per date and rounded to
the nearest KES 500. Campaign overlays sit on top: festive 1.5, August holidays
1.25, April holidays 1.15.

**Guest-facing prices are always quoted per break, never per night**, because
checkout prices per break and the unit must not change mid-funnel.

From-price for a three-night break, at the floor:

| Lodge | 3 nights | 4 nights | 7 nights |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cedar 2 bedroom | KES 84,000 | 112,000 | 196,000 |
| Cedar 3 bedroom | KES 126,000 | 168,000 | 294,000 |
| Signature 2 bedroom | KES 114,000 | 152,000 | 266,000 |
| Signature 3 bedroom | KES 168,000 | 224,000 | 392,000 |

Season card from-prices, cheapest real product in each season, a three-night
Cedar 2 bedroom break: long rains from KES 84,000, short rains from 90,000,
cool season from 96,000, sunshine season from 109,500. These are unchanged by
anything in this document, because the entry rate stays at 28,000. If it ever
moves, the card prices in scripts/seed-cms.ts must be recomputed the same day.

The footnote promise stays: "lowest price for a three-night Cedar Lodge
2 bedroom break in the season."

Deposit, balance and cancellation are unchanged and live in
docs/deposit-and-cancellation-plan.md. Nothing here touches them.

## 12. Voice and grounding

Everything in DESIGN.md holds: warm, family-first, British English, no em
dashes, "from KES X" with the footnote. Added or amended for Mount Kenya:

- **Ground in real places.** Mount Kenya, the Burguret, Naro Moru, Nanyuki, the
  A2, the equator. Never invent geography.
- **Wildlife is a neighbour, behind a fence, and the fence is honest.** Colobus
  and sykes monkeys, tree hyrax at night, bushbuck, Hartlaub's turaco,
  silvery-cheeked hornbills, sunbirds, crowned eagles, trout in the rivers.
  Elephant and buffalo beyond the boundary, which is exactly why there is one.
- **Trees by name.** Cedar, podo, wild olive, fig, bamboo. The tone is a
  neighbour who knows what grows here.
- **Seasons are always named with their months.** No card or page uses the
  northern seasons, ever.
- **School holidays are the demand language.** "The August holidays", never
  "the summer break".
- **Local languages are seasoning.** Kirimara for the restaurant, karibu on the
  village page welcome, and nowhere else. No phrasebook copy.
- **Altitude and cold are assets, not caveats.** Say "bring a jumper" with
  pleasure.
- **Never Center Parcs assets, text or photography, in any form.**
